# 925,000 photos, sorted by rangers and AI

## Problem

Indigenous rangers in Australia's Warddeken Indigenous Protected Area run camera traps to monitor wildlife — but 70 to 95 percent of the roughly 925,000 images come back empty: wind, rain, leaves. Sorting them by hand took six months per survey round, stealing time from actual land management.

## Solution

MegaDetector, from Microsoft's AI for Good Lab, scans camera-trap images and finds animals, people, and vehicles — filtering out the blanks so rangers only review images with something in them.

## Artifact

- Code: https://github.com/microsoft/MegaDetector — the detector itself: model, pip-install quickstart, and docs (67 commits, active)
- Open license: MIT (declared LICENSE file, verified 2026-10-14)

## Human

The bininj and daluk rangers of Warddeken Land Management in West Arnhem Land, working with Charles Darwin University and CSIRO. Published October 2026 in Conservation Science and Practice: images needing review fell from 925,088 to 172,290 — an 81.4% reduction — and processing time dropped from six months to two. The freed time went to species identification, language documentation, and workshops. The tool is now used by more than 80 conservation organizations worldwide.
