# Seven days' warning before the water comes

## Problem

Floods affect an estimated 95 to 240 million people a year, killing 6,000 to 8,000 and causing up to $33 billion in damage — and in the most vulnerable regions, warnings arrived too late or not at all. In Bangladesh, floods kill an average of 5,000 people a year.

## Solution

Google's Flood Hub uses neural-network models to forecast river flooding up to 7 days ahead across 100 countries, publishing hyper-local inundation maps that show predicted flood depth and extent down to the village level. Alerts go out through Search, Maps, and partner networks including the Red Cross.

## Artifact

- Live site: https://sites.research.google/floods — zoom into live inundation maps, pick any covered river area, see current and forecasted flood depth and extent
- Code: https://github.com/google-research/flood-forecasting — the open-source models behind Flood Hub
- Open license: Apache-2.0 (declared LICENSE file, verified 2026-10-14)

## Human

700 million people across 100 countries now receive 7-day flood warnings. The Red Cross and GiveDirectly use the forecasts to trigger cash transfers before floods hit. A Yale-partnered study found communities with local volunteers were 50% more likely to receive alerts before water reached their area — "a factor that can make a life-or-death difference." Project lead: Yossi Matias, Google's VP of Engineering and Crisis Response.
