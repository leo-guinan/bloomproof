# The open map of every protein

## Problem

Figuring out a protein's 3D shape used to take months of lab work per protein — and shape determines function, which determines drugs. For underfunded diseases like melioidosis, a bacterial "silent killer" claiming around 90,000 lives a year mostly in Southeast Asia, that slowness meant treatments stalled for lack of basic structural knowledge.

## Solution

AlphaFold predicts a protein's 3D structure from its amino-acid sequence in seconds, at accuracy rivaling lab experiments. The predictions are published in a free searchable database of more than 200 million structures covering nearly every known protein.

## Artifact

- Live site: https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk — searchable database of 200M+ predicted protein structures; type any protein, see its shape, download the data
- Code: https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphafold — the open-source implementation
- Open license: Apache-2.0 for the code (declared LICENSE file, verified 2026-10-14); CC BY 4.0 for the database content (stated on the database site, verified 2026-10-14)

## Human

Dr. Su Datt Lam's team at the National University of Malaysia, using AlphaFold to map the proteins of the melioidosis bacterium and accelerate new drug development. Broader reach: more than 3 million researchers use the database, and over 30% of AlphaFold-related research now targets neglected diseases like malaria and leishmaniasis.
