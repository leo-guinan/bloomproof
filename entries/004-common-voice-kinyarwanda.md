# A voice for 12 million speakers

## Problem

None of the big voice assistants understand a single native African language. For Kinyarwanda's 12 million speakers — many with low literacy or visual impairments — the entire voice-technology revolution simply didn't exist in their language.

## Solution

Mozilla's Common Voice crowdsourced more than 1,700 hours of donated Kinyarwanda speech into a public-domain dataset. Rwandan AI startup Digital Umuganda built speech-to-text and text-to-speech on it — including Mbaza, a chatbot that gives citizens government and health information in Kinyarwanda, spoken aloud.

## Artifact

- Live site: https://commonvoice.mozilla.org — listen to clips, donate your own voice, download the datasets (250+ languages)
- Code: https://commonvoice.mozilla.org — the CC0 voice datasets download from the same platform
- Open license: CC0 public domain for the voice datasets (declared in the project's own README, verified 2026-10-14)

## Human

Kinyarwanda speakers in Rwanda — including blind users locked out of text-based information. More than 840 Rwandans donated the founding 1,700 hours after a Kigali hackathon. Audace Niyonkuru, CEO of Digital Umuganda: "with speech technology, no one will be left behind."
